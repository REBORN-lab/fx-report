## Themes of the week

### Theme 1: the dollar side changed its pricing anchor twice inside the span, landing on the Treasury's buyback plan (affects USD, EUR)
**Macro backdrop**: the span opens with attributable evidence of cooling hike bets (2026-08-18), turns mid-week on the release of the July 28–29 FOMC minutes and an official's "if inflation does not come down, a hike is needed" (2026-08-20), and ends with reporting naming the dollar the biggest loser from the Treasury's latest buyback plan (2026-08-21). Across all three anchor changes the policy rate of 3.625 and the real rate of 0.26 never moved once.
**Transmission**: the Treasury's buyback arrangement → the term compensation earned by marginal allocators holding Treasuries thins → those allocators shift the increment back to the other four legs (capital flows) → the exchange rate. The bid is being reset by the same marginal buyers, not by a new rate differential: the rate leg stood still throughout, what moved is what those buyers will pay for the same differential.
**Target impact**: the euro leg is -0.907% on the week with a range of 0.85477–0.86386, and the last fixing at 0.85477 sits exactly on the range floor; it ranks second of the four, with the baht the largest.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on three days — cooling hike bets on 2026-08-18, the minutes and the official's hike language on 2026-08-20, the buyback plan on 2026-08-21 — and is closed out by the four same-signed fixing of 2026-08-22. Contrary evidence is on the record too: on 2026-08-19 the same batch had the 30-year yield touching a 19-year high, and on 2026-08-21 another item said the minutes show a September hike still on the table — both point to the rate leg still having pricing power.
**Key assumption**: pricing weight has already moved from "does September hike or not" to "supply and buyback arrangements on the Treasury side", while the policy rate of 3.625 and the real rate of 0.26 never moved once across those three anchor changes. If the assumption fails, then neither rates nor the Treasury side is what moved, the euro's -0.907% is the euro's own affair, this theme loses its transmission starting point, and the dollar row falls back to having no local driver.
**Alternative reading**: the three anchor changes are just narrative rotation, and what actually moved is each counterpart itself — on 2026-08-20 euro-area yields fell back from multi-year highs, with the US Treasury announcement given as the cause but the impact landing on the euro-side curve; on 2026-08-22 the euro leg also picked up an official reading of its own. Its flip indicator: at the next fixing the euro weakens versus 0.86386 while the dollar strengthens against the other three at the same time — once that appears, what moved is each leg itself and there is no unified push on the dollar side.
**Flip indicator (deadline 2026-09-16)**: the policy rate moves away from 3.625 — once that appears, pricing weight has returned to the rate leg and the buyback line gives way. No T+N here: the test point is the FOMC of 2026-09-16 on the calendar.

### Theme 2: the dollar's giveback is not distributed evenly across the four counterparts, and the peso is the only leg running the other way (affects EUR, PHP, THB, BRL)
**Macro backdrop**: within the span the four local currencies no longer move with the same sign against the dollar: the baht -1.06%, the euro -0.907% and the real -0.716% all move toward stronger local currency, while the peso at +0.245% moves toward a weaker peso. In the previous weekly these four still carried the same sign.
**Transmission**: the marginal allocation giveback on the dollar side → each counterpart absorbs its share of that giveback according to its own capacity (capital flows) → the exchange rate. The absorbers are the marginal buyers of each local-currency asset; on the peso leg that absorption is crowded out by a local external gap — the record lows of 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-20 are both attributed to oil prices extending their rise, and 2026-08-21 adds the July balance of payments swinging back to deficit.
**Target impact**: the last fixings of the baht at 32.675–33.065, the euro at 0.85477–0.86386 and the real at 5.1729–5.2102 each land exactly on their own range floor; the peso's last fixing of 61.66 sits mid-range within 61.509–61.821, at +0.245% on the week and pointing the other way.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the two peso record-low days of 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-20 and the balance-of-payments deficit day of 2026-08-21, and gets its sharpest split on 2026-08-22, when three legs printed range floors together and the peso was the only one that did not. Contrary evidence is on the record: at that fixing all four in fact shared a sign and the peso was merely the smallest — the inversion is a cumulative result over the span, not something true at every fixing.
**Key assumption**: the three same-signed legs come from one common denominator (the giveback on the dollar side) and the peso's inversion comes from a local gap — the dividing line falls precisely between the peso's +0.245% and the negative signs of the other three. If the assumption fails (the three are in fact independent), this theme is just three things colliding at once and the euro, baht and real rows in the landing table have no shared invalidation condition. One zero-effect judgement also has to be stated outright: the offline digital-euro standards consultation and the ASEAN financial-risk cooperation item within the span do not constitute a directional effect on exchange rates, because neither falls on rate differentials, risk premia, capital flows or policy space.
**Alternative reading**: the ordering of the distribution is really each leg's own rate-differential position, not a common dollar giveback — the baht at -1.42, the euro at -0.499 and the real at 9.359 sit at very different real-rate depths, and the ordering of magnitudes partly lines up with that. Its flip indicator: at the next fixing the ordering of the four magnitudes lines up completely with the ordering of -1.45, -1.42, -0.499 and 9.359, with the peso out in front — once that appears, each leg's own differential position is doing the pricing and the common-denominator layer does not hold.
**Flip indicator (T+3)**: at the next fixing the peso carries the same sign as the other three — once that appears, the peso's inversion does not come from a local gap and the "uneven distribution" layer lapses.

### Theme 3: for the two economies with no room left on the rate leg, the whole adjustment is pressed onto the exchange rate (affects PHP, THB)
**Macro backdrop**: Thailand's policy rate is 1.0 with a real rate of -1.42; the Philippines' policy rate is 4.75 with a real rate of -1.45. Both are negative real rates, Thailand's nominal rate is already hugging the floor, and its weekly move of -1.06% is the largest of the four.
**Transmission**: no room to cut the nominal rate → an external shock cannot be absorbed on the rate leg → it lands in full on the exchange rate (policy space) → the exchange rate. Those bearing it are holders of local-currency bonds and deposits: they get no compensation from rate repricing, and the only exit they can move is the exchange rate.
**Target impact**: the baht's weekly range is 32.675–33.065 with the last fixing at 32.675 being the range floor itself, and that day's move of -0.729% is also the largest of the four; the peso's weekly range is 61.509–61.821 at +0.245% on the week, pointing the other way.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the three days of 2026-08-19, 2026-08-20 and 2026-08-21 where the baht section wrote policy space as the dominant variable, on the two-source deviation of 0.699 breaching its threshold on 2026-08-20, and on 2026-08-22 where the baht printed the range floor and brought the deviation in to 0.077. Contrary evidence sits inside the same theme: the deepest real rate of the five is the Philippines' -1.45, and its weekly move is the only positive one of the four at +0.245% — if "no room on the rate leg" really meant "the exchange rate bears it in full", the deepest leg should have travelled furthest.
**Key assumption**: 1.0 and 4.75 both stay put until their respective test points. If the assumption fails (the MPC of 2026-08-26 or the Monetary Board of 2026-08-27 moves the rate), the adjustment share returns to the rate leg on the spot and this theme lapses immediately.
**Alternative reading**: the two are really running their own local stories — the peso on oil prices and the external gap, the baht on dovish expectations — and the shared "no room on the rate leg" is a coincidence, with the -1.45 and +0.245% pair as the evidence for it. Its flip indicator: after the two decisions of 2026-08-26 and 2026-08-27 land, the two legs still point in opposite directions — once that appears, merging them into one layer was wrong.
**Flip indicator (deadline 2026-08-26)**: the policy rate is cut from 1.0 — once that appears, the baht leg still has room on rates and the exchange rate is not the only exit. No T+N here: the test point is the MPC of 2026-08-26 on the calendar.

### Theme 4: the leg with the thickest carry cushion, compounding in the same direction as the dollar's giveback (affects BRL)
**Macro backdrop**: the real's real rate of 9.359 is the highest of the five economies, and the policy rate of 14.0 never moved within the span. Its weekly move is -0.716% with a weekly range of 5.1729–5.2102.
**Transmission**: the highest real rate → carry longs earn the thickest holding return on this leg → incremental buying goes to local-currency assets first (rate differential) → the exchange rate. The bid comes from the carry positions themselves, which is not the same money as the marginal buyers doing allocation giveback in Theme 2.
**Target impact**: the real at -0.716% with a weekly range of 5.1729–5.2102 and a last fixing of 5.1729 that is the range floor itself; its magnitude ranks third, behind the baht's -1.06% and the euro's -0.907%.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the three days of 2026-08-18, 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-20 that wrote 9.359 as the dominant variable from standing facts, on the Chicago trading record of 2026-08-21, and on the range floor printed on 2026-08-22. Two things inside the theme run against it: the magnitude does not lead, ranking third rather than first; and the two-source deviation of 0.657 on 2026-08-22 (previously 0.028) breached its threshold, putting this leg's price evidence below the other three at that fixing.
**Key assumption**: 14.0 does not step down again before the COPOM of 2026-09-16. If the assumption fails, the 9.359 cushion thins, this theme loses its transmission starting point and the real row falls back to being a passive share of Theme 2.
**Alternative reading**: -0.716% is only the passive share the dollar giveback handed it, unrelated to the carry cushion — that is, it belongs entirely to Theme 2 with no layer of its own. Its flip indicator: at the next fixing the gap between the real and the baht and euro magnitudes converges into one band with all three carrying the same sign — once that appears, it has no independent pricing source.
**Flip indicator (deadline 2026-09-16)**: the policy rate steps down again from 14.0 — once that appears, the carry cushion is narrowing and this theme's direction reverses. No T+N here: the test point is the COPOM of 2026-09-16 on the calendar.

## What changed in last week versus the week before

- Theme 1 (the dollar's pricing anchor moved to the buyback arrangement): direction changed — the previous report this line was "one and the same inflation print starts two opposite paths at once", and this report it is reclassified as pricing weight having left the inflation print; what overturned it is the 2026-08-21 passage, where the minutes showing a September hike still on the table and the dollar being named the biggest loser appear on the same day, while the next day's fixing had all four moving the same way toward stronger local currency — the hike path did not take the weight.
- Theme 2 (uneven giveback, peso inverted): evidence changed — the four legs of the previous report's "four local currencies move against the dollar with the same sign" are down to three same-signed this report; the evidence that replaced it is the peso, which set record lows on 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-20, added the July balance of payments swinging back to deficit on 2026-08-21, and was the only one of the four not to print a range floor when the other three did on 2026-08-22.
- Theme 3 (the leg with no rate room bears it on the exchange rate): evidence changed — the previous report this line stood on the baht alone, and this report the peso is merged into the same layer (policy rate 4.75, real rate -1.45); direction and flip level did not move, what changed is the supporting evidence, and half of the new evidence runs the other way, with the peso leg not following.
- Theme 4 (the leg with the thickest carry cushion): direction changed — the previous report the real was "the one leg that does not fit", and this report it moves with the baht and the euro while the leg that does not fit has become the peso; it no longer argues from "being unlike the others" but from the 9.359 cushion it owns, while the 0.657 deviation breach took its evidence down a notch.
- The previous report's Theme 3 (the euro carrying an extra "done hiking" layer, inseparable from the shared leg) is gone this report: the euro is no longer the largest move, its layer is explained from both ends — Theme 1's anchor change on the dollar side and its own official reading — the inseparability question is no longer carried by the euro alone, and no independent observable is left.
