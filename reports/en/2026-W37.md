## Themes of the week

### Theme 1: the hike actually landed, and the euro weakened against the dollar twice in a row after it (affects EUR, USD)
**Macro backdrop**: the ECB decision of 2026-09-10 lifted the policy rate to 2.5% with hawkish guidance, and in the second half of the covered span the framing shifted from "coming" to "more to come" — policymakers reportedly opened the door to further hikes on energy risk, and Barclays reportedly expects another hike in December.
**Transmission**: expectations of more hikes → risk premium → the tightening is read as a drag on growth, holders of euro assets demand more compensation, and the books that had priced it early close out that stretch of duration once it lands → the exchange rate. The leg doing the work is cross-border allocators demanding more compensation, not the policy rate itself.
**Target impact**: the euro's weekly change was 0.258%, weekly range 0.85822–0.86266; it weakened against the dollar twice in a row after the landing, and the span's last fixing at 0.86266 is exactly the weekly range high.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the decision landing on 2026-09-10 and the escalating framing of 2026-09-11 and 2026-09-12. The contrary item is 2026-09-12, when the Bank of France governor reportedly voiced concern about the state of the French economy — that points at the growth side, not the tightening side.
**Key assumption**: the pair of the euro-area policy rate 2.25 (period 2026-09-08) and CPI 2.942874 (period 2026-07) does not change before the next update, so -0.693 stays put; if it fails, the compensation layer is recomputed on the new pair, the premise "the hike has not yet reached the compensation layer" disappears, and this theme has to change its call.
**Alternative reading**: these two weakenings are not the euro demanding more compensation but the dollar side bidding after the higher inflation print; its own flip: at the next fixing the baht breaks further above 33.065 while the euro does not follow above 0.86266 — the push is on the dollar side and the euro has no selling of its own.
**Flip indicator (T+3)**: at the next fixing the euro breaks back below 0.85822 — once that appears, this theme is reversed: the post-landing selling has been cleared.

### Theme 2: the US August CPI print thinned the real leg, which makes the thickest cushion of all the more worthwhile (affects BRL, USD)
**Macro backdrop**: the US CPI release of 2026-09-11 falls inside the covered span — CPI year-on-year 3.397 (period 2026-08) against a previous 3.365, and the US real rate 0.228; on the Brazilian side 9.557 (policy rate 14.0 period 2026-09-08 − CPI 4.4430246091 period 2026-07) never moved across the span.
**Transmission**: the inflation print rises → rate differential → the nominal leg does not move while the real leg thins, the gap to 9.557 actually widens, and carry books add back the exposure they had unwound → the exchange rate. The leg doing the work is the carry books' re-entry, not any Brazilian domestic reading.
**Target impact**: the real's weekly change was -0.298%, weekly range 5.0889–5.1261 — the only one net stronger against the dollar inside the covered span; the span's last fixing at 5.1108 turned back toward its own currency from the previous fixing's 5.1247 and is still inside the 5.0889–5.1261 range.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the CPI landing and the 0.228 reading of 2026-09-12 and on the real's contrary notch the same day. The contrary item is 2026-09-11, when the real weakened 0.703% against the dollar right up to just under the range high — on that day the thickest carry cushion followed the dollar instead.
**Key assumption**: Brazil's policy rate 14.0 (period 2026-09-08) does not change before COPOM of 2026-09-16, so 9.557 stays put; if it fails, the thickness of the cushion is recomputed, the premise "the gap actually widens" falls away, and the step this theme rests on is broken.
**Alternative reading**: the real's firmness is not a widening real differential but the real being sold into at the range high; its own flip: at the next fixing the real breaks further below 5.0889 while the euro at that same fixing still breaks above 0.86266 — the dollar side has not retreated and the real firms anyway, which says the move is Brazil's own.
**Flip indicator (T+5)**: at the next fixing the real breaks back above 5.1261 — once that appears, this theme is reversed: the widening real differential never converted into price.

### Theme 3: the two Asian legs parted ways — the baht handed its exhausted policy room to the exchange rate while the peso stood still (affects THB, PHP)
**Macro backdrop**: Thailand's policy rate 1.0 (period 2026-09-03) is the lowest of the five economies with a real rate of -0.947; the Philippine real rate -1.1 (policy rate 5.0 period 2026-09-04 − CPI 6.1 period 2026-08) is the deepest negative of the five, and neither moved across the span.
**Transmission**: easing room compressed to the lowest of the five → policy room → foreign positions have no new easing bet left to place and the direction is ceded to the external dollar bid → the exchange rate. The leg doing the work is cross-border books buying dollars back, which the foreign position in local bonds cannot absorb.
**Target impact**: the baht's weekly change was 0.517%, weekly range 32.875–33.065 — the largest of the four; the peso's weekly change was -0.019%, weekly range 62.491–62.641 — all but standing still.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on 2026-09-11 and 2026-09-12, when the baht printed a new range high on both days. The contrary group is inside the theme itself: on 2026-09-12 the peso reportedly hit a new low against a strong dollar, yet its weekly change is still negative — same region, no follow-through.
**Key assumption**: Thailand's policy rate 1.0 (period 2026-09-03) and CPI 1.947079 (period 2026-07) do not change before the Bank of Thailand meeting of 2026-10-28, so -0.947 stays put; if it fails, easing room reopens, the whole "no bet left to place" premise collapses, and this theme has to be rewritten on the rates leg.
**Alternative reading**: the baht's notch is not exhausted policy room but the same dollar-side move it shares with the peso, only with more elasticity; its own flip: at the next fixing the baht breaks further above 33.065 and the peso at that same fixing breaks further above 62.641 — the two back in step, which says what moved is the common denominator, not local policy room. Separately, the Thai item on escalating measures against illicit financial activities does not constitute a directional influence on the exchange rate: it falls on none of rate differentials, risk premium, capital flows or policy room, and this theme does not build on it.
**Flip indicator (T+3)**: at the next fixing the baht breaks back below 32.875 — once that appears, this theme is reversed: exhausted policy room was never priced into the exchange rate.

### Theme 4: inside one week the four moved in lockstep and then split — the common denominator held for exactly one fixing (affects USD, EUR, PHP, THB, BRL)
**Macro backdrop**: mid-span all four moved the same way toward their own currencies and each printed a range floor (the 2026-09-10 daily); the next day all four turned the same way toward the dollar (2026-09-11); at the last fixing the real went the other way and the formation broke up (2026-09-12).
**Transmission**: the bid on the dollar side → capital flows → cross-border books adjust all four local-currency exposures at the same fixing, the most elastic moving first and the thickest cushion exiting last → the exchange rate. The leg doing the work is the books' synchronized rebalancing, not any country's domestic reading.
**Target impact**: the weekly changes do not agree — the euro 0.258% and the baht 0.517% weaker against the dollar, the peso -0.019% and the real -0.298% stronger toward their own currencies; and the whole round trip stayed inside each weekly range: 0.85822–0.86266, 62.491–62.641, 32.875–33.065 and 5.0889–5.1261.
**Evidence strength**: it stands on the target impact of 2026-09-10, 2026-09-11 and 2026-09-12. The contrary item sits at the end of the span — on 2026-09-12 the real parted from the other three and the common denominator failed at the last fixing.
**Key assumption**: the push from the dollar side is still the four's common denominator and local readings only set the elasticity — the ranking of the five real rates never moved inside the span, Brazil's 9.557 the thickest and the Philippines' -1.1 the thinnest; if it fails, the four price off their own local sides, this theme loses its explanatory power, and the conjunction calls in the landing table have to be split apart.
**Alternative reading**: the lockstep in the middle of the span was coincidence and what actually moved was each currency's own local story; its own flip: at the next fixing the euro breaks further above 0.86266 while the baht instead breaks below 32.875 — the two parting ways says there is no common denominator.
**Flip indicator (T+5)**: at the next fixing all four move the same way again, the euro breaking above 0.86266 and the real above 5.1261 at that same fixing — once that appears, this theme is reversed: the common denominator still governs all four.
